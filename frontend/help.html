Help

Occasions Address Book keeps the mailing list for your cards and invitations. Each household is one envelope; each occasion is a list of who it's for.

Signing in

Accounts are by invitation. The invitation email has your sign-in email and a temporary password; the first time you sign in you choose your own password (at least 8 characters).

  • Remember my email on this device fills in your email next time.
  • Forgot password? emails a 6-digit code that works for an hour.
  • Whenever your password changes, you get an email saying so. If you didn't change it, use Forgot password? right away.
  • For extra safety, turn on two-step verification with an authenticator app in Settings › Security.

After 30 minutes without using the app, it signs you out. Two minutes before, Still there? appears; Stay signed in keeps anything you were in the middle of. Using the app in another tab counts too.

Households

Add one with + Household or by pressing N on the Address book. The edit window has four tabs; Next walks through them, and Save keeps the changes on every tab.

Greetings

  • Household Greeting (required) is the name on labels and envelopes, like "The Smith Family".
  • Formal Greeting is for formal envelopes, like "Mr. and Mrs. John Smith". When the Informal Greeting has two first names, it's filled in for you as "Mr. and Mrs. Last name".
  • Informal Greeting is how emails and invitations open, like "Dear John and Mary,".

Address

The mailing address. Leave Country blank for the US. When address checking is set up, Check address looks the address up in the Post Office's address data (through Google), says whether mail is delivered there, and offers the corrected version (with ZIP+4) to use or keep. Save the household with a confirmed address (an apartment counts when the Post Office confirms the building but doesn't list its apartments) and its card shows a green check mark in the corner (under the note icon, if there's a note), until the address is changed. There's a monthly allowance of checks for everyone using the app; when it's used up, the button turns off until the next month.

People

First and last name, email and phone for each person. Phone numbers tidy themselves into (248) 555-0101 when you leave the box. Typing two names like "Peter and Paula" in one First box makes two people with the same last name.

Tick Default on the person who should get emails for the household. If several people share an email address, put it on each of them.

Groups & Notes

Groups (Family, Neighbors, Work…) help you filter and build lists; start typing to reuse a group you already have. Notes are just for you; a household with a note shows a small i on its card that shows the note when clicked.

Finding people

Search looks through names, streets, cities and emails. Filter narrows the list by several groups (in any or all of them), cities, states, the start of a ZIP code, whether there's an email or a complete mailing address, addresses that passed Check address (Verified), and addresses waiting for your review. Each filter shows as a chip you can remove; Clear all resets them. The same Filter is on each occasion's list.

Importing, exporting and sharing a household

Import… and Export… are in the account menu (your name at the top right).

Importing contacts (.vcf)

In Contacts on a Mac, select the people you want (or a list) and choose File › Export › Export vCard…; on an iPhone, share a contact. Google Contacts and Outlook can export vCards too. Drag the file onto the Import window (or click it to choose the file): people at the same address become one household, like John and Mary Smith becoming "The Smith Family". Untick any you don't want. Find narrows the list, and Select all or Select none apply to what's shown.

Importing a spreadsheet (.csv)

Save the list as CSV from Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets. The app guesses what each column holds from its heading; check them, step through the rows with ‹ ›, then import.

Contacts you already have

A contact that's already in your address book (the same Household Greeting, the same street and ZIP, or one of the same people by name, email or phone) is marked Merges with existing and isn't added twice. Instead it fills in only what that household is missing: a phone or email someone doesn't have yet, a person who isn't listed, an address when there's none (or a missing apartment, city, state or ZIP on the same street), and blank greetings, groups or notes. Anything your address book already has stays as it is.

Exporting

Export the whole address book as a spreadsheet (.csv), which imports back exactly with each person's email and phone in the People column, or as contacts (.vcf) for Contacts, Google Contacts or Outlook.

Sharing one household

The share icon at the top right of a household's edit window sends that household as a vCard (one contact per person) or a one-row CSV, through AirDrop, Mail or Messages, or as a download.

Keeping addresses current

Confirm address (on a household's edit window) emails them a private link, good for 30 days, to confirm or correct their address. You get an email when they answer. A changed address waits for you: the household shows New address, and its Address tab shows what they sent beside what's on file, to Use new address or Keep the one on file. Nothing changes until you choose.

The household's card shows where things stand: Address check sent while you wait, Address confirmed once they say it's still right or you use what they sent, and No reply to address check if the link runs out first.

Occasions

An occasion is one mailing or invitation, like "Christmas cards 2026". Its type decides what you can do with it:

  • Cards or Labels: print labels or envelopes and track who was mailed.
  • Invitations: the same, plus an invitation you design and print, one page per household.
  • Emails: an email you design, sent to the people you choose.

For cards and invitations, Address Greeting Style picks the first line of each label, envelope and postcard back: the Household Greeting ("The Smith Family"), the Formal Greeting ("Mr. and Mrs. John Smith") or the Informal Greeting ("John and Mary"). A household without the chosen one gets its Household Greeting. Invitations also have an Invitation Greeting Style, the greeting inside the card ("Dear John and Mary"), and Emails an Email Greeting Style, which can also be the first names of the people it goes to.

Tick households to put them on the list, or narrow the list with search and Filter and use Add all shown to the list. The duplicate icon at the top right of an occasion's card makes a copy named "Copy of …" with the same settings, design and list, with nobody marked mailed or sent yet, so next year's cards begin with this year's. Rename it with Edit. Not mailed yet shows who still needs one.

Printing

Print… on an occasion first asks what to print, then what to print it on; only stock that suits the choice is listed. Who picks the households (not mailed yet, everyone on the list, or the ones shown) and Order sorts them by last name or ZIP. The greetings come from the occasion (Edit): Address Greeting Style for the first line of each address, and Invitation Greeting Style for the greeting inside each invitation.

Print… opens the pages in a new tab along with your browser's print window. Always print at Actual size / 100%, never "Fit to page". To keep a copy instead, choose Save as PDF in the print window.

Invitations

For Invitations occasions, once the invitation is designed. Each household gets its own card with its greeting.

  • Avery 8387 postcards: 4¼ × 5½ in, four to a Letter sheet. Print on Letter paper; most printers can't print right to the edge, so a background color may stop just short of the perforations.
  • 4 × 6 in postcards, 5 × 7 in cards, half Letter and Letter: one card per page, so choose that paper size in the print window.

Orientation prints the invitation Portrait (tall) or Landscape (wide). A single card prints on a landscape page; on Avery 8387 the sheet goes in the same way and each design is turned a quarter in its place, so hold the cards sideways to read them. Your choice is remembered.

On postcards, Print the address on the back adds each household's address (and your return address, if you like) on the back of its own card: each sheet's back follows its front. Print two-sided, flipped on the long edge. With a printer that can't print two-sided, print the odd pages, put the sheets back in turned over, and print the even pages; try one sheet first to learn which way your printer wants them. Households without a complete address are skipped. A 4¼ × 5½ postcard mails at the postcard rate.

Addresses

  • Avery 5160 / 8160 labels, 30 per sheet. Start on label number uses up a partly used sheet.
  • A2 envelopes (5¾ × 4⅜ in) fit 4¼ × 5½ cards like Avery 8387; A7 envelopes (7¼ × 5¼ in) fit 5 × 7 cards. One envelope per page, with your return address in the corner if you like. In the print window choose that envelope's paper size and feed envelopes the way your printer's tray shows.

Households without a complete mailing address are skipped, and you'll see who.

Return addresses

Your return address, set in Settings › Return address, on:

  • Avery 5195 / 8195 / 18294 return address labels (1¾ × ⅔ in, 60 per sheet), or Avery 5160 / 8160 address labels: the rest of a sheet, from Start on label number.
  • A2 or A7 envelopes with only the return address, to address by hand; How many sets the number of envelopes.

On label sheets, choose a theme from Styles and click one of its designs, shown with your own return address. The window remembers your choice.

  • Everyday: Plain, Monogram (the first letter of your last name in script down the left side), Script, Flourish, Laurel, Bold surname, Frame
  • Holiday: Wreath, Christmas tree, Mistletoe, Santa Claus, Candy stripe, Snowy pines, Snowflake, Polka-dot ribbon, Starry night
  • Patriotic: Stars & stripes, Bunting, Star-spangled, Americana
  • Spring: Tulips, Cherry blossoms, Butterfly
  • Summer: Beach, Sunflower, Watermelon
  • Fall: Autumn leaves, Pumpkin, Flannel
  • Fun: Confetti, Balloons, Rainbow

The same designs fit both label sizes. Designs with color print best on an inkjet printer; print one sheet on plain paper first to check how it lines up.

To print an envelope's address and return address together, choose Addresses on envelopes with Return address in the corner.

For a first try, print on plain paper with Draw outlines on and hold it against the labels, envelope or postcard sheet up to the light. If labels or envelopes land a little off, nudge them in Settings › Printer and print the alignment test page again.

Once they're in the mail, Mark mailed ticks everyone you just printed.

The designers

Open the Invitation Designer or Email Designer from Edit on the occasion. Whatever you see is what prints or arrives.

  • Font, Text color, size, bold, italic, underline: with words selected they change those words; with nothing selected they change the whole design.
  • Each color square opens a color window: the current color and its code, the colors already in your design (to match one exactly), a palette, a box for a color code, and More colors….
  • Insert field adds a placeholder filled in for each household: Greeting, Occasion name, Date, Time, Location or Your name.
  • Line adds a divider. Click it to change its style (including calligraphy flourishes), color or length.
  • Picture adds a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP up to 5 MB, or drop one from your Mac onto the page. Click a picture to place it beside the text or centered, size it, give it a border, or remove it; drag it to move it within the text.
  • Background and Border set the page color and a frame around it.
  • </> shows the HTML, to edit or paste your own. Anything unsafe is removed when you save.
  • Undo and redo (⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z) cover every change.
  • Preview shows the design filled in for each household on the list; ‹ › steps through them.
Fancy fonts like Great Vibes always show in the app, on printed invitations and in Apple Mail. Gmail and Outlook show a similar standard font instead; colors, sizes, pictures and layout look the same everywhere.

Sending emails

On an Emails occasion's list, each household shows its people who have an email address. Tick who gets it: just Mary, both, or the whole family. Ticking the household ticks its default contact.

The Greeting setting in Edit decides how each email opens: the Informal, Formal or Household Greeting, or the first names of the people ticked. So an email sent only to Mary can still say "Dear John and Mary".

Send emails… shows the email, who each one goes to, and lets you change a household's greeting. Choose Not sent yet or Everyone with email, try Send a test to me, then Send. Replies come to your own email. Each household shows Sent or Failed afterwards.

Sharing your address book

In Settings › Sharing, share your address book and its occasions by email, as Can edit or View only. Someone without an account gets an invitation. Everyone keeps their own address book too; the switcher at the top of the screen moves between yours and the ones shared with you.

  • Can edit: everything except changing who it's shared with.
  • View only: look, search, export and print, but no changes and no emails.
  • Shares follow the person's account, so either of you can change your sign-in email without sharing again.

Two people at once

Changes the other person makes show up on your screen within a minute, or as soon as you come back to the window. If you both edit the same household, occasion or design, the second Save asks whether to Load their version or Save mine anyway.

Settings

  • Profile: your photo, name, the name emails come from, and your sign-in email (changing it emails a code to the new address; the old one works until you enter it).
  • Return address: printed on envelopes and return-address labels. It belongs to the address book, so everyone using a shared book prints the same one.
  • Security: change your password, and two-step verification with an authenticator app.
  • Printer: nudge labels and envelopes into place, with test pages.
  • Sharing: who your address book is shared with.

Trash

Deleted households and occasions go to the Trash (in the account menu) for 30 days, then disappear for good. Restore brings a household back onto the occasion lists it was on, and an occasion back with its list. Delete forever and Empty trash are there too.

Admin

Admins see Admin in the account menu. It lists every account, with its status: Active (has signed in), Invited (hasn't chosen a password yet) or Turned off.

  • Invite creates an account and emails a temporary password. The invitation is good for 30 days; Resend invite sends a fresh one.
  • Turn off signs the person out everywhere and stops them signing in. Nothing is deleted, and Turn on lets them back in. Books they share stay shared.
  • Delete permanently removes the account with its address book, occasions, designs, trash and photo. Anyone that person shared with loses the shared book, and they lose the books shared with them. It can't be undone.

You can't turn off or delete your own account. Admins are added with scripts/make-admin.sh and need to sign out and back in once to see the menu item.
